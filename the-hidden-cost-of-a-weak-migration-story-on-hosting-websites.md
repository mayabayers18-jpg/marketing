<p>The Hidden Cost of a Weak Migration Story on Hosting Websites<br>
![](https://jpcdn.it/img/small/668691ac1e0442d88c98c82990a604f0.png)</p>

<p>Every hosting or cloud infrastructure company eventually runs into the same sales objection: prospects are already hosted somewhere else, and switching feels risky. Most hosting website templates never address this directly — they focus entirely on the new customer signing up fresh, and quietly ignore the much larger segment of buyers who are trying to leave a competitor.<br>
This is a real gap, because migration friction is often the actual reason a deal stalls, not price or features. A page that only says "get started free" is speaking to the wrong audience half the time.<br>
The fix is straightforward but rarely implemented well: a dedicated section addressing migration directly, ideally with credits, training resources, or hands-on technical support built into the offer. This single addition can shift a page from feeling like a beginner-only signup form to a serious infrastructure partner capable of handling complex transitions.<br>
Migration messaging works best when it's paired with the rest of a trustworthy structure — real product categories (apps, databases, storage, routing), uptime and data protection claims, and clear support signals in the navigation. Without that foundation, a migration CTA on its own won't do much.<br>
For anyone building or auditing a hosting company site, it's worth studying how one template handles this balance. A full walkthrough of a hosting website template covers exactly where the migration messaging succeeds and what else needs to be built around it — including the pricing gap that still needs solving.<br>
If your hosting site doesn't speak to the buyer who's already hosted somewhere else, you're likely losing more deals than you realize.<br>
